# Planning a dental implant: what a 3D scan can tell your dentist

A dental cone-beam CT scan, or CBCT, helps your dentist assess the available jawbone and plan where an implant can sit in relation to nearby structures.

## What your dentist looks for on the scan

Your dentist can look through slices of the jaw to measure the bone at the proposed site. In the lower jaw they may check the position of the nerve canal. In the upper jaw they may look at the floor of the sinus. They also consider where the final crown needs to sit so that it fits your bite and can be cleaned.

## Could a scan change the treatment plan?

The findings can affect implant size or position, or prompt a discussion about bone grafting or a different restoration. The dentist combines the scan with an examination, your health history and the plan for the final tooth before recommending treatment.

## Do I need a scan at the first visit?

A conventional dental X-ray may help with the initial assessment. CBCT is recommended for three-dimensional assessment before implant surgery. It uses X-rays and typically delivers a higher dose than conventional dental X-rays. If you already have relevant images, bring them to your consultation and ask whether they can be used. The dentist can explain what question a new scan would answer and choose a scan area appropriate to the case.
