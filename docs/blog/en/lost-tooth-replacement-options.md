# Missing a tooth? Compare a bridge, an implant and a denture

A fixed bridge, an implant crown and a removable partial denture each have trade-offs. Compare surgery, healing time, effects on nearby teeth and daily care.

## When a fixed bridge makes sense

A conventional bridge fills a gap with an artificial tooth attached to crowns on neighbouring teeth. It may be worth discussing if those teeth already need crowns and can support the bridge. When they are healthy, ask how much tooth structure would need to be removed. For some front teeth, a resin-bonded bridge may be an option and may need less preparation.

A tooth-supported bridge usually avoids surgery. Ask the dentist to show you how to clean underneath it and explain what happens if one of the supporting teeth needs treatment later. See [fixed dental bridges](/en/services/prosthetics#bridges).

## What an implant changes about the treatment plan

An implant is placed in the jaw and can support a crown without preparing the neighbouring teeth. It involves surgery and time for healing. The schedule depends on bone and gum health, implant stability and whether bone grafting is needed. If the gap is visible when you smile, ask what you can wear while the area heals and when the final crown is likely to be fitted.

An implant-supported bridge can also replace several teeth. Ask how many implants the planned restoration would need. See [dental implant placement](/en/services/surgery-implantation#implantation) and [implant-supported restorations](/en/services/prosthetics#implant-prosthetics).

## When a removable denture is worth considering

A removable partial denture can replace one or more teeth. You take it out to clean it, and there may be an adjustment period for speech and eating. Ask what will hold it in place, how it will feel and what follow-up adjustments may be needed. When all the teeth in one jaw are missing, options can include a complete denture, an implant-retained removable denture or a fixed implant-supported arch. See [removable dentures](/en/services/prosthetics#removable-dentures) and [full-mouth rehabilitation](/en/services/prosthetics#full-mouth-rehabilitation).

## What to ask at your consultation in Tbilisi

Tell the dentist which matters most to you: avoiding surgery, closing a visible gap quickly, keeping the neighbouring teeth intact or making daily cleaning manageable. Mention any medicines you take, relevant health conditions and whether you smoke. Ask for a breakdown of the proposed plan covering imaging, any grafting, the temporary tooth, the final restoration and follow-up.

If you’re fitting treatment around time in Tbilisi, ask which stages can be combined in one visit, which need a healing interval and where you’ll have follow-up care.

At Total Charm Dent, the dentist examines your mouth and discusses the findings with you. Depending on the case, they may use clinical photographs, take an intraoral scan or a conventional impression, or recommend imaging. See the clinic’s [restorative dentistry services](/en/services/prosthetics).

## Common questions

### Do I need one implant per missing tooth?

Several teeth may be restored with a bridge supported by fewer implants. The dentist works out how many supports are needed after assessing the site and the restoration.

### How long does implant treatment take?

The time between implant placement and the final crown is often several months. Ask for a timeline based on your examination, including any treatment needed before implant placement.

### Can I leave the gap for now?

That depends on the location of the gap and how your teeth function. Bone can shrink after extraction and neighbouring teeth may shift. If an implant is a possibility, an early consultation can help you understand your options and timing.
